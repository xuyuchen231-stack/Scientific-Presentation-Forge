# Talk structure and timing

| Mode | Narrative and required content | Typical pacing |
|---|---|---|
| PhD seminar | Scientific problem, concrete challenge, research question, mechanistic/data-driven/hybrid rationale as relevant, platform/method, findings or proposed evaluation, next steps | About 8–12 main slides for 10–15 minutes; extend to fit the actual seminar |
| Conference | Problem, precise gap, contribution, minimum necessary method, 2–3 key results, implications, conclusions | Reserve roughly half of the talk for results and interpretation |
| Paper report | Bibliographic context, question, approach, central figures, interpretation, strengths, concrete follow-up | Explain the source paper's work separately from the presenter's analysis |
| Proposal | Problem, evidence for gap, hypotheses, work packages, experimental design, success criteria, feasibility, timeline, expected contribution | Clearly distinguish preliminary results from planned work |
| Defense | Thesis question, contribution map, methods, validated results by contribution, synthesis, implications, future work | Prepare backup slides for assumptions, controls and examination questions |

Use claim titles in results and explanation slides. Purpose/section titles are acceptable when there is no established result. A proposal title must not present an expected outcome as an observation. Put one main exhibit on each results slide, with the interpretation adjacent to the relevant feature. Split dense figures into successive views without changing their quantitative meaning.

Allocate seconds per slide and account for transitions and opening/closing. Keep a small timing buffer. Prefer familiar, pronounceable words in speaker notes and use the same technical terms as the slides. Do not insert a history section unless it advances the scientific argument. Retain formulas when they explain the model, but move lengthy derivations to backup slides.

Default design: generous margins, approximately 28–32 pt titles, 20–24 pt body, 16–18 pt chart labels and 12–14 pt citations. Honor the user's explicit 16/18/20 pt preference where requested, and check the projected result. Let figures occupy enough space to read their labels. End with three concrete conclusions and their implications. Keep contact information there if supplied.
